      Footnote · How the numbers on this page are worked out

      Framework scoreBusiness plan measure, student list, all-tests summary

      RuleThe three tests sit on one ladder of 300 points. Foundation is 0–100, Year 1 is 100–200 and Year 2 is 200–300. A student's framework score is where their test starts on the ladder plus their percentage on it.

      Example65% on Foundation is 65. 40% on Year 1 is 140. 72% on Year 2 is 272.

      Framework growthBusiness plan measure, student list, summary numbers

      RuleFramework score at the student's last assessment of the year minus their framework score at their first. A student needs 2 assessments in the year to be counted.

      Example55% then 75% on Foundation is 55 → 75, so +20. A student who passes Foundation with 92% and then scores 40% on Year 1 goes 92 → 140, so +48. Passing shows as progress, not as a drop.

      Business plan measureYear group and school reports

      RuleAverage framework growth for every student at a year level, whatever test they sat. Year level comes from the student's own record, not their class or their test.

      Business plan measure by assessmentBy assessment, year level × assessment

      RuleEach student's year counts under the test they started the year on: their first assessment that year. Their framework growth includes any progress after passing it, because getting a student through a test is that test's job. “Average change on this test” is the percentage change for students who sat it in both semesters. “Passed it” counts every student who scored 90% or more on it that year.

      ExampleA Year 3 student on Foundation scores 80% in Semester One, passes with 93% in Semester Two and moves to Year 1. Their +13 counts under Foundation. Another Year 3 student scores 35% then 52% on Year 1, so 135 → 152 is +17 under Year 1.

      Change on the same testStudent list, test summaries

      RuleOnly worked out when a student sat the same test at their first and last assessment of the year: last percentage minus first. Students who passed and moved up show “Passed” instead, because their percentage restarts on the new test.

      Colour bandsResults by colour band, student list

      RuleThe same bands as the scoring sheets. Yellow is 90% or higher, which is a pass. Green is 50–89.9%. Red is under 50%.

      PassedSummary numbers, school and program tables

      Rule90% or higher on a test. The student sits the next test at their next assessment. Passing Year 2 completes the framework.

      Question progressQuestion progress

      RuleFor one test: the average share of marks on each question in Semester One and in Semester Two. Only students who sat that test in both semesters are counted, so both dots describe the same students.

      Teaching targetsShared teaching targets, student list

      RuleEvery question below full marks on the student's latest assessment. A shared teaching target is a question that's a target for 2 or more students on the same test. The 6 questions shared by the most students are shown.

      No gainStudents not moving upStudent list, Students not moving up

      RuleFramework growth of 0 or less across the year. Program administrators see how many, never who.

        RuleFor each year level: the average framework growth of every student who had 2 assessments that year, whatever test they sat. “On each test” shows which test those students were on at their latest assessment.

        Secured rateQuestion secured rates, hardest questions

        RuleCounts students who sat the same test in both semesters and had less than full marks on the question in Semester One. The secured rate is the share of them with full marks in Semester Two. Students who passed in Semester One move to a new test, so they aren't counted for the old one.

        Example6 students sat Foundation in both semesters and dropped marks on “Subtract” in Semester One. 4 had full marks in Semester Two, so the secured rate is 4 ÷ 6 = 67%.

        Hardest questionsHardest questions

        RuleThe 5 questions on the chosen test with the lowest secured rate that year, from questions with at least 3 students counted.

          Footnote · How the flags on this page are worked out

          !!!Question signalsQuestion signals by school

          RuleA question is flagged for a school when all three of these are true:

          1. At least 5 of the school's students were counted for its secured rate. With 3 or 4, one or two stuck students can set off flags across a whole test.
          2. The school's secured rate is lower than the whole program's.
          3. The gap is too big to put down to chance. Suppose the school's students were exactly as likely to secure the question as the program average. What's the chance of a result this low, or lower, just by luck? Below: less than 1 in 10. Well below: less than 1 in 50.

          How the chance is worked outIt's a one-sided binomial test, the same as the FSA reports. With n students counted, k secured, and a program rate of p, the chance is the sum, for every i from 0 to k, of C(n, i) × pi × (1 − p)n − i.
